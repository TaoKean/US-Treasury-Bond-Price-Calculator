Methodology

Spot-rate discounting

A bond is a set of future cash flows. Each coupon and the final principal payment is discounted using the spot rate for its own maturity, not a single yield for the whole bond. The Federal Reserve publishes the parameters of a smooth curve (the Svensson form) for each date. From them, the calculator gets the continuously compounded spot rate z(t) at the exact time t of every payment, then:

discount factor = exp(−z(t) × t) present value = cash flow × discount factor

The model dirty price is the sum of the present values. Because the curve is evaluated at each payment’s exact time, fractional years work, and so do payments due in under one year.

Clean and dirty price

The dirty (full) price is what a buyer would pay in total for all the remaining cash flows. Part of the next coupon has already been earned by the seller. That earned part is accrued interest, based on the actual days in the current coupon period. The clean price is the dirty price minus accrued interest, and it is the price convention used for quotes. On a coupon date, accrued interest is zero and the two prices are equal.

Prototype assumptions

  • Regular semiannual coupons, with dates counted back from maturity in six-month steps. Dates that fall on a short month use that month’s last day, without drifting afterward.
  • The valuation date is treated as the settlement date. Only payments strictly after it are included.
  • Time to each payment is the actual number of calendar days divided by 365 (Actual/365 Fixed).
  • The valuation date must exist in the dataset. Prices are not extrapolated past the longest maturity the Federal Reserve published for that date.
  • Rows are dropped when the curve parameters they need are missing. The BETA3 term is skipped when BETA3 is zero, as in the early years of the data.

What the result is not

The price comes from a fitted curve, so it is a model value. It will not match actual market quotes exactly, and it ignores taxes, trading costs, and the differences between individual securities. This is a coursework prototype, not investment advice.
